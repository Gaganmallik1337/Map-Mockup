1. Game Overview

A persistent, browser-based geopolitical simulation set on a real-world map. Each player is a citizen of a country.

Citizens train to grow stronger, work for companies (extracting raw materials or manufacturing finished goods),

fight in battles, vote in elections, run for office, write for newspapers, post on a social network, chat with allies,

and trade on a global marketplace.

1.1 Core principles

• 

One real-world day = one game day. The world reset happens at 00:00 GMT every day. All daily

counters, HP refills, and time-based ticks align to this clock.

• 

Every meaningful action is performed by a real player. The only exception is admin-created NPC

accounts (described in §49.7), which exist solely to seed empty newly-unlocked countries.

• 

There is no end state. Players progress along multiple ladders (strength, work skill level, military rank,

wealth, political office, login streak, social following) that grow indefinitely.

• 

The only formal, named positions in the game are: Citizen, Congress Member, President, Party President

(Party Leader), Military Unit Commander. Everything else (owning a company, owning a newspaper,

fighting in battles, trading on markets, posting on Pulse) is an activity a citizen does, not an identity they

hold.

• 

The game runs on a single server. Multi-server support is not in scope for v1.

• 

Gold is sold for real money. Gold is the monetization currency; players buy it with real currency. This is

documented fully in §42.

2. The Map & Geography

2.1 World map

The map is a real-world geographic map showing all real-world countries. Continents and country borders match

real-world geography.

Special note on India: The map displays Jammu & Kashmir, Aksai Chin, and Arunachal Pradesh fully as part of

India, using boundaries from the Survey of India's official data. A disclaimer is shown on the map page noting that

the map is based on Survey of India data and the game world is fictional.

2.2 Countries

Every real-world country exists on the map. Each country has:

• 

A fixed, distinct color that fills all of its regions. The color is a property of the country, not the regions.

• 

Exactly 7 regions per country, fixed at world creation. The region count is permanent — admins and

congress can rename regions but cannot add, remove, merge, or split them. The 7-region structure is

uniform across all countries to support consistent map design.

• 

A unique local currency (e.g., India → INR, USA → USD).

• 

A capital region, set by admin at world creation (defaulting to the real-world capital location). Congress

can pass a "Move Capital" law to change it later.

2.3 Regions

Each region within a country has:

• 

A name (editable — see below).

• 

An optional resource assignment: Iron, Grain, Oil, or None.

• 

Map overlay icons for active battles and a capital marker.

Region renaming:

• 

In-game: Congress passes a "Rename Region" law. Any congress member can propose. 24-hour voting

window, simple majority required, no Gold cost. The new name must pass validation (1–40 characters,

profanity filter applies). On the law passing, the name updates immediately on the map and in all

references.

• 

Admin override: Super Admins can set or override any region's name at any time from the admin panel.

2.4 Resources — fixed binary system

There are exactly 3 resources in the game: Iron, Grain, Oil. Each region either has one of these three resources,

or none. There are no high/medium/low tiers — a region either matches a resource type or doesn't. Binary.

Resource bonus mechanic (applies to Raw Material Companies ONLY):

3. Country States & Activation

3.1 Locked vs. unlocked countries

All real-world countries are drawn on the map. However, countries do not start playable. They begin in a locked

state.

A locked country:

• 

Is visible on the map (drawn with country shape and borders) but rendered with a distinct visual treatment

(greyed out / "Locked" overlay).

• 

Cannot be selected during registration as a country of citizenship.

• 

Cannot be traveled to.

• 

Has no government, no congress, no president, no political parties, no companies, no Military Units.

• 

Cannot be attacked, signed into MPPs, joined into coalitions, or interacted with in any economic way.

An unlocked country has all of these capabilities enabled and is fully playable.

3.2 Admin unlocking

Super Admins unlock countries one at a time from the admin panel. There is no fixed schedule — the admin

decides when to unlock each country.

3.3 Adjacency with locked countries

The system computes country neighbor relationships dynamically. Locked countries are skipped in adjacency

calculations.

Example: If Pakistan is locked, India and Iran are treated as direct neighbors. India can declare war on Iran, travel

to Iran is direct, and MPPs between India and Iran are valid. When Pakistan is later unlocked, adjacency

recomputes: Pakistan becomes a wedge between India and Iran, and they are no longer direct neighbors unless

they share another active border.

3.4 Unlocking a country — bootstrap and grace period

When an admin unlocks a country, the country starts completely empty: no government, no congress, no

president, no parties, no companies, no MUs.

30-day grace period. For the first 30 days after unlock:

• 

The country cannot be attacked. No war declarations can be made against it.

• 

The country cannot declare war on other countries either.

• 

The country cannot be set as a Natural Enemy by other countries.

• 

The country can receive citizenship applications, host companies, allow MUs and parties to form, and

participate in market trade normally.

3.7 Grace period ends

30 days after unlock, the grace period expires. The country becomes fully open to attack, MPP, coalition, Natural

3.8 Auto-locking on depopulation

Auto-locking is determined solely by citizenship count, not by physical presence. If a country's count of

citizens (people whose citizenship is that country) drops to 0 — all citizens migrated away or accounts deleted —

the country auto-locks. It becomes inaccessible until a Super Admin manually unlocks it again. Empty regions

remain on the map.

A foreigner who is merely physically located in a country does not keep it unlocked. If a country auto-locks while

foreign visitors are standing in it, those foreigners are immediately relocated to their own home country's capital

region.

unlocked country simply becomes selectable at registration, travelable, and open for citizenship,

4.3 What a citizen can do

Subject to level, citizenship, and resource constraints documented elsewhere, a citizen can: train (once per day);

work (once per day, if employed); eat food (up to 20 times per day); fight in battles; travel; buy and sell on the

Product Market; trade on the Monetary Market; buy and sell companies; accept job offers; create raw and

manufacturing companies; create or join a party (level-gated); create or join an MU (level-gated); create a

newspaper (level-gated); post on Pulse; participate in chat; comment on newspaper articles; send private

messages; make friends; block citizens; donate currency or items to citizens, country treasury, or MU; vote in

elections; run for congress, party leader, or president (membership-gated); propose and vote on laws (if a congress

member); apply for citizenship in another country; report players or content; buy Gold with real money; and

voluntarily delete their account.

7. Health (HP) System

HP is the single, unified resource used for all player actions that consume effort. There is no separate "energy"

stat.

7.1 HP rules

• 

Baseline HP cap: 100. At daily reset (00:00 GMT), every citizen's HP is set to exactly 100, regardless of

what it was before.

• 

HP can exceed 100 during the day by eating food. Any HP above 100 at the moment of daily reset is wiped

— HP cannot be banked across days.

• 

HP cannot go below 0. Actions that would cost more HP than the citizen has are blocked with an error

message.

• 

There is no passive HP regeneration. Only the daily reset and eating food restore HP.

9. Work, Work Skill & Jobs

9.1 Work skill is a LEVEL (not a raw number)

Every citizen has a work skill level (1, 2, 3 …), independent of strength. It grows only by working — never by

training. A citizen who never works stays at level 1 regardless of how much they train. Work skill level directly

affects how much a company produces when this citizen clicks Work.

9.2 The Work page and the Work action

The dashboard Work button is always visible, regardless of employment, and routes to the Work page (also

reachable via My Places → Work). The Work page always opens, even when the citizen has no job:

• 

If employed: the page shows the job — employer's company (name, type, quality, region), the salary, the

citizen's work skill level + progress chart, and the Work button (one click, free / 0 HP). After working it

shows the salary earned (net of work tax), the working-day credited toward the next level, the XP gained

(+5), and a "Work complete — come back tomorrow" state with a countdown.

• 

If unemployed: the page still opens and displays the citizen's current work skill level and progress, plus

a "You don't have a job yet" notice and a button to the Job Market (pre-filtered to jobs the citizen qualifies

for, with a "show all jobs" toggle).

• 

If the employer (manufacturing) has no raw materials: a message — "Your employer has no raw

materials today; you couldn't work" — plus a button to the Job Market.

• 

Hard Worker medal progress (e.g., "17 / 30 works until your next Hard Worker medal") is shown.

Working: pays the salary in the company's local currency (after work tax — §13); credits a working day toward

the next work skill level; produces goods/raw materials for the company (to the owner's inventory by default —

§10); awards +5 XP (once per day; subsequent work attempts blocked).

9.3 Work skill level thresholds

Levels are earned by accumulating total working days. Each early level doubles the requirement; from level 8

onward it is a flat 64 days per level.

9.9 One job per citizen

A citizen can hold only one job at a time. To change jobs, they quit their current job and accept another.

9.10 Job Market

The Job Market lists every open job offer across all unlocked countries. Each offer shows: company name (with

link), company type (e.g., "Q3 Weapons Factory" / "Q2 Iron Mine"), country and region, salary in the company's

local currency, minimum work skill level required, number of positions open, and an optional description.

Citizens browse and filter by country, salary range, company type (raw vs manufacturing), and minimum work

skill level (showing only offers they qualify for).

9.11 Accepting a job — auto-accept, no owner approval

When a citizen clicks "Accept Offer": the system checks the citizen meets the minimum work skill level. If yes,

they become an employee immediately (no owner approval). If no, an error is shown. The owner cannot reject

applications. The owner can: set the minimum work skill level when posting; fire employees at any time (no

cooldown, no notice); edit the salary, description, position count; and close the offer manually. A job offer auto-

closes when all positions are filled (the owner can re-open or post a new offer).

9.12 Quitting a job

A citizen can quit at any time, no cooldown, no penalty. They can immediately accept another job.

9.13 Out-of-raw-materials block

If a manufacturing company's owner lacks enough raw materials for the worker's potential output, the worker is

blocked: error — "This company has insufficient raw materials. You cannot work here today." No salary, no XP,

no work skill credit that day. The citizen can quit and accept another job immediately. The owner keeps raw

materials stocked by: owning a raw company (vertical integration); buying on the Product Market; or receiving

donations from citizens, country treasury, or MU.

9.14 No work tiers, boosters, or minimum wage

There are no work facility tiers and no work boosters; working is one flat action per day. (No Premium account

exists, so no Premium work bonus.) There is no minimum-wage law — companies set salaries freely; market

dynamics decide what attracts workers.

9.15 Hard Worker medal

Every 30 works, the citizen receives a Hard Worker medal (+5 Gold reward — §40). Counter resets after each

award.

9.16 Click flows — the job market, accepting & quitting

Finding a job — the Job Market. Open Marketplace → Jobs tab, or from the Work page when unemployed

click Job Market (it opens this tab pre-filtered to offers you qualify for, with a 'show all jobs' toggle — §9.2).

Each row shows company name (link), type (e.g., 'Q3 Weapons Factory'), country + region, salary in the

Accepting an offer. Click a row → the offer detail opens → click Accept Offer. The system checks your work-

a confirmation toast shows, My Places → My Job now points to this company, and the dashboard/Work page

flips to the employed state. Fail → an inline error ('Requires work skill level N') and you are not hired. If you

Quitting. Open My Places → My Job (or the Work page) → Quit Job → confirm → you are unemployed

accumulated working-day progress are unaffected.

Blocked at Work (no raw materials). When a manufacturing employer lacks raw materials, the Work page

replaces the Work button with 'Your employer has no raw materials today; you couldn't work' plus a Job Market

button (§9.13). No salary, XP, or working-day credit is granted that day; you may quit and accept elsewhere

A citizen of one country can own a company located in another country. The company is rooted in its host country

10.9 Owner responsibilities

The owner posts job offers; fires employees at any time; decides what to do with produced goods (sell, hold,

donate, distribute, use as input); maintains raw material supply (manufacturing); pays creation/upgrade costs in

Gold; can sell the company on the Companies for Sale tab; and can donate the company to an MU or a country

(one-way, irreversible). The owner cannot accept/reject job applications — they are auto-accepted if the applicant

meets the work skill level threshold (§9.11).

The owner then decides what to do with the goods.

10.11 Where raw materials come from for manufacturing

A manufacturing company consumes raw materials from the owner's inventory. The owner keeps enough

available via: vertical integration (owning a raw company); market purchase; or donation (from citizens, country

treasury, or MU). If raw materials are insufficient when an employee clicks Work, the employee is blocked

(§9.13) and the company produces nothing that work-action.

10.12 Listing currency for company-produced goods

When the owner lists company-produced goods on the marketplace, the listing is priced in the owner's home

country currency — regardless of where the goods were produced. (An Indian owner lists in INR even if the

goods were made in Pakistan.) For MU listings: the MU's home country currency. For country/government

listings: the country's own currency.

10.13 Government-owned companies

A country can acquire a company three ways: (1) Build from scratch — congress passes a "Found Government

Company" law specifying type, quality, region, and initial Gold cost; (2) Buy on the open market — the

president (or a delegated congress member) buys a listed company within an "Authorize Government Company

Acquisition" budget; (3) Accept donations — citizens may voluntarily donate a company to the government.

There is no forced nationalization.

Management: the president (or delegate) executes operations within a congress-authorized budget ("Authorize

Government Company Operations" law) — salaries, firing, raw material supply, goods disposition. Salaries are

paid from the country treasury in the country's own currency; if it runs out, salaries can't be paid and employees

can quit. Selling/privatization: congress passes a "Sell Government Company" law; the president lists it on

Companies for Sale; proceeds go to the treasury. Foreign government ownership: allowed; host-country

economic laws apply.

All government market and company operations are performed from the Country page's Treasury Market

& Operations section by the president or an authorized congress delegate (see §11.10 and §24).

10.14 MU-owned companies

A citizen can donate a company to an MU (one-way, irreversible — it becomes MU property). MU-owned

companies are managed by MU members holding the Manage Companies privilege. Goods/raw materials

produced go to MU storage; salaries are paid from the MU's treasury currency. All MU company and market

operations happen from the MU page (see §11.11 and §20).

10.15 Conquered regions and companies

When a region's ownership changes (one country conquers it in war), companies in that region: stay put (rooted

in the region, do not migrate); keep their current owner; and tax flow redirects to the new ruling country (work

tax now flows to the conqueror).

10.16 No company limits

There is no hard cap on the number of companies a citizen, country, or MU can own. Gold creation costs and

ongoing salary/raw-material obligations are the natural friction.

10.17 Selling companies on the marketplace

A company owner can list their company on the "Companies for Sale" tab (§11.8). Company listings are in Gold

only (no local currency for company sales).

Lists entire companies offered for sale.

12. Currencies, Treasury & The World Bank

12.1 Currencies

Two kinds of currency exist:

1. 

Gold — universal, scarce currency for major purchases (company creation, MU creation, citizenship fees,

war declaration, MU upgrades, company purchases, Name Change, Pulse promotion, etc.). Gold is

purchasable with real money (§42).

2. 

Local currencies — one per country (INR, USD, EUR, …). Used for salaries, product purchases, and

country-level economic activity.

12.2 Wallets

Every entity holds a multi-currency wallet: a citizen holds Gold + any number of local currencies; a country

14. Travel

14.1 Why travel

A citizen can move from one region to another (potentially across countries). Travel is required to: fight in a battle

outside the citizen's current allied region (§15); start a resistance war (must be physically in the contested region

— §18). Most other activities (work, market, messaging, Pulse, chat) do not require travel — they work remotely.

14.2 How travel works

To travel, a citizen must: (1) hold a ticket (Q1–Q5); (2) choose a destination country (from currently-unlocked

countries); (3) choose a destination region (one of the 7); (4) click Travel — the ticket is consumed and HP is

deducted. No distance/range mechanic — every region is reachable in one step from anywhere.

14.3 Travel to conquered regions

When a region has been conquered, it belongs to the conquering country. To travel there, select the current

controlling country as destination. In that country's region list, the conquered region appears with both its current

owner and an original-owner label, e.g., "Kashmir (originally India — currently controlled by Pakistan)." Click

and travel normally. A citizen of the rightful owner wanting to start a resistance war in their occupied region uses

this same process.

15. Combat & Battles

15.1 Battle structure

Every battle has the same structure:

• 

15 rounds total.

• 

Each round lasts 60 minutes (1 hour).

• 

Battles end when one side wins 8 rounds (so battles last between 8 and 15 hours total).

15.2 Round mechanics

Within a round, both sides accumulate damage as their fighters attack. At the end of the 60-minute round, the side

with more total damage wins the round. Ties go to the defender. Damage resets to 0:0 at the start of each new

round — no carryover.

15.3 Battle win

The first side to win 8 of 15 rounds wins the battle. The battle ends immediately upon the 8th round win.

15.4 Attack actions

A citizen permitted to fight (see §15.7) can perform two attack actions:

• 

Fight — one attack, costs 10 HP, deals one damage roll. +1 XP per fight. Consumes 1 weapon.

• 

Berserk — combined 5 attacks in one click, costs 50 HP, deals 5 independent damage rolls summed into

one big hit. Awards +25 XP total per Berserk. Consumes 5 weapons.

16. Battle Replay System

16.1 Purpose

A replay is a showcase system — it lets players and MUs demonstrate how they performed in a battle. It is social

proof ("watch how our MU carried this battle," "see my Battle Hero round") and feeds directly into Pulse sharing

and the competitive/bragging culture that drives engagement.

16.2 What a replay is

Every completed battle can be watched as a replay. A replay is not a compressed summary of the whole hour —

instead, each round's segment is the literal final 30 seconds of real gameplay from that round (the 0:30 → 0:00

window), shown at normal speed exactly as it happened. The per-round clips are stitched back-to-back across

every round that was played, ending with a final results screen. A full 15-round battle = ~7.5 minutes (15 × 30s); a

battle won in 8 rounds = ~4 minutes (8 × 30s).

Every round that was played gets its last-30-seconds clip — regardless of whether anything notable happened in

that window (a quiet round still shows its real final 30 seconds).

16.3 What each 30-second round segment shows

• 

Every hit that actually landed in the final 30 seconds — all the real "PlayerX hit for 4,200" / "PlayerY

berserked for 18,500" events as they occurred in that window. Because 30 seconds of real time is a small,

bounded volume, all hits in the window are shown faithfully — there is no "notable hits only" curation.

• 

The damage race — both sides' damage totals, opening at the real standing totals at the 30-seconds-

remaining mark (e.g., "India 4.2M – Pakistan 4.0M"), then climbing as the final real hits land.

• 

The round result — which side won the round, flashed at the end of the segment.

• 

Running round score (e.g., "Rounds: India 3 – Pakistan 2").

16.4 Final results screen

Winner + final round score; all Battle Heroes (the top damage dealer of each round, up to 15); top 10 overall

damage dealers of the whole battle; region outcome (conquered / defended); total damage by each side.

16.5 Faithful reconstruction

The replay uses the actual recorded hits and exact damage totals from the final 30 seconds of each round —

everything shown really happened, at real speed, with no alterations. The standing totals at the start of each clip,

every hit in the window, and the round result are all exact and real.

16.6 Playback controls

Play / pause; a round scrubber (jump to any round); and a speed toggle (1× / 2× / 4×). This lets a viewer skip to

the decisive round.

17. Wars

17.1 Declaring war

A country can declare war on another country only if:

• 

The target shares a border (locked countries skipped — §3.3).

• 

The target is not within its 30-day post-unlock grace period (§3.4).

• 

The president of the attacking country proposes a "Declare War" law (only the president can propose this

specific law — it is never delegatable).

• 

30 Gold is escrowed from the attacking country's treasury when the law is proposed.

• 

The attacking congress passes the law (24h voting, simple majority).

If the law passes, the 30 Gold is consumed and war begins. If it fails, the Gold returns.

17.2 War initiative

Inside an active war, only the country holding initiative can start the next battle. A country has initiative when (1)

there is no active battle in this war right now, AND (2) the country has not lost a battle in this war in the past 24

hours. If 24 hours pass without the initiative-holder starting a battle, initiative is lost and passes to the other side.

Declaring war does not itself grant initiative — initiative is unowned at the start; the first country to start a battle

takes it.

17.3 Starting a battle within a war

The country with initiative chooses a region of the opposing country to attack. The region must be adjacent to the

attacker's current territory (or to a region already conquered in this war). A battle then runs per §15.

Who can start a battle: only the sitting president of the country with initiative. Starting a battle is not

delegatable (same restriction as the "Declare War" proposal — it stays president-only). If the president is offline,

the war stalls — no one else can open the next battle, and initiative passes to the enemy after 24 hours of

inactivity (§17.2).

Click path (president only):

1. 

The president opens the Country page → "War Operations" section (visible only to the president).

2. 

The panel shows the war's initiative status. If the country holds initiative, the "Start Battle" button is

active; otherwise it is greyed out with the reason ("Enemy holds initiative" / "You lost a battle in the last

24h" / "A battle is already active in this war").

3. 

The president taps "Start Battle" → a target-region picker opens.

4. 

The president picks the enemy region to attack. Only regions adjacent to the country's current territory

(including regions already conquered this war) are selectable; non-adjacent enemy regions are shown but

locked.

5. 

The president confirms. There is no Gold cost to start a battle — the 30 Gold was paid at the war

declaration.

6. 

The battle goes live for everyone: the country now holds initiative, the Round 1 clock starts, and the battle

appears on the Battles page (§15.12) and as a map overlay for all players to fight in.

Feature Blueprint — Final

The active war is also surfaced on the War and Politics page (§45.9) for everyone's visibility, but the Start Battle

action itself lives only on the Country page.

17.4 Sequential battles only

Only one active battle at a time in any given war.

17.5 Cumulative conquest

As regions are conquered, the attacker's territory expands. The next battle can be on any region of the opponent

now adjacent to the attacker's expanded territory.

17.6 Ending a war

A war ends when either:

1. 

Mutual cease-fire. Either country's congress passes a "Sign Peace" law. If passed, the other country has 7

days to reciprocate by passing its own peace law. If both pass, the war ends. If 7 days pass without

reciprocation, the offer expires and the war continues.

2. 

Total conquest. One country loses all its regions. The war auto-ends; the conquered country enters

"occupied" status (§17.7).

17.7 Occupied country status

If a country loses all its regions: the country still exists in the database — its citizens remain citizens of it; it has

no government, congress, president, or operating parties until at least one region is liberated. Citizens of an

A resistance war (RW) is a special battle that allows the rightful owner country of an occupied region to attempt to

18.2 Requirements to start an RW

A citizen can start an RW if: the targeted region is currently held by a foreign country; the citizen is a citizen of

18.3 Cooldown

• 

Congress members of the rightful owner country: can start an RW immediately after the previous one

ended.

• 

Non-congress citizens: must wait at least 15 minutes after the most recent RW in their country ended.

18.4 Battle mechanics

The RW battle follows the same 15-round / 60-minute structure as regular battles (§15). Both sides can fight

(same rules as §15.7).

18.5 Outcome

• 

If the rightful owner wins: the region returns to the rightful owner. The RW initiator receives 20 Gold + a

Resistance Hero medal (counted on profile, §40).

• 

If the occupier wins: no region change. The initiator's 20 Gold is forfeit.

3. 

President election — elected by the 7 seated congress members (parliamentary). Candidates are eligible

22.5 ELECTION 2 — Congress (vote on the 13th)

The structure. Each country has 7 congress seats = its 7 named regions (one seat per region). Each seat is

literally "the seat for [Region]." It is a per-region first-past-the-post contest (not D'Hondt, not flat plurality).

To run (candidacy, 9th–11th): An eligible citizen goes to Country → Elections → Congress (or My Party),

president, and their party must be a top-5 party. Free to run. They join their party's pool of congress candidates.

Party Leader assigns seats (the 12th): Each top-5 party's Party Leader goes to My Party → Congress List and

assigns up to one member per seat — choosing which member contests which region's seat. A member can be

assigned to any region's seat regardless of where they physically live. A party may contest any subset of the 7

seats: if a leader assigns candidates to only 4 seats, that party appears in only those 4 seats.

To vote (the 13th, 24 hours): All eligible citizens vote on all 7 seats. For each of the 7 seats, the ballot shows

that seat's candidates (the candidate each top-5 party assigned to that seat). The voter picks one candidate per

seat (so up to 7 selections total), then confirms. Cannot vote for self. Counts hidden.

Results (the 14th): Each seat is won by the candidate with the most votes for that specific seat. A seat with one

candidate → that candidate wins unopposed. A seat with zero candidates → the seat stays empty that term

(congress simply has fewer members). Ties within a seat → earliest candidacy registration. Congress = the 7 seat-

winners. Each winner: +50 XP + Congress Member medal, takes office on the 14th, and may propose and vote

on laws for the term.

22.6 ELECTION 3 — President (vote on the 24th, by congress)

To run (candidacy, 21st–23rd): Any eligible citizen can run for president — not just congress members.

Eligibility: party member ≥ 7 days, citizen ≥ 30 days, level 10+, and pays the 10 Gold candidacy fee (admin-

editable). The voter goes to Country → Elections → President, clicks "Run for President," and writes a

campaign presentation (editable until voting starts). Dual-office rule: a sitting congress member cannot run for

president, and the president cannot run for/sit in congress — so a presidential candidate is by definition someone

who did not contest/win a congress seat this cycle. The "Run" button is disabled with a clear reason if the citizen

holds the conflicting office.

To campaign (21st–24th): Candidates post on Pulse and may spend Gold to promote posts (§30); a "Presidential

Candidates" page lists all runners with their presentations.

To vote (the 24th): The 7 seated congress members are the electorate. Each congress member casts one vote for

one eligible presidential candidate. Majority of 7 = 4 votes to win. Cannot vote for self.

23. Congress & Laws

23.1 What congress does

Congress is the elected legislative body of a country (7 members). Members propose and vote on laws, and elect

23.2 Proposing a law

Any congress member can propose any law except "Declare War" (president only). When a law involves

spending money or Gold, funds are escrowed from the country treasury at proposal. If the law fails, funds return;

if it passes, funds are consumed.

23.3 Voting on a law

One vote per member per law (yes / no / abstain). 24-hour voting window. Simple majority of yes-votes among

voters wins (abstentions don't count). Supermajority (66.6%) required for: "Declare Natural Enemy" and "Join

Coalition."

26. Mutual Protection Pacts (MPPs)

26.1 What an MPP is

A bilateral defense agreement between two countries. An active MPP grants citizens of both countries a +10%

damage bonus when fighting on the side of the MPP partner in any battle.

26.2 Signing an MPP

Mutual signing is required — both countries' congresses must pass the "Sign MPP" law within 7 days of each

other:

1. 

Country A's congress proposes "Sign MPP with Country B." 40 Gold escrowed from A.

2. 

Congress votes (24 hours).

3. 

If passed, the proposal sits as "pending reciprocation" — Country B has 7 days to pass its own "Sign MPP

with Country A" law (B pays its own 40 Gold).

4. 

If B reciprocates within 7 days: the MPP activates; both 40 Gold are consumed.

5. 

If B does not reciprocate: A's 40 Gold is refunded.

Each country pays its own 40 Gold (total per MPP signing = 80 Gold across both).

26.3 Duration

60 days from activation.

26.4 Auto-renewal

Either country's congress can toggle auto-renewal on/off (via "Renew MPP"). When on, 40 Gold is auto-deducted

from that country's treasury at expiry, extending the MPP another 60 days. Insufficient Gold at auto-renewal:

the MPP auto-cancels with notification to both countries' congresses and presidents.

26.5 Pre-expiry notification

24 hours before expiry, a notification is sent to both countries' congress members and presidents.

26.6 Early cancellation

Either side's congress passes "Cancel MPP." Cancellation includes a 7-day grace period during which the +10%

bonus still applies. After the grace, the MPP ends. No refund.

26.7 Compatibility

Multiple MPPs simultaneously are allowed. Compatible with coalitions and Natural Enemy designations (a

country can even have an MPP with its Natural Enemy).

42. Real-Money Gold Sales

42.1 The monetization model

Players buy Gold with real money. Gold is the monetization currency; it is used for everything Gold is used for

(company creation/upgrades, MUs, parties, war declaration, MPPs, citizenship fees, Name Change, Pulse

promotion, etc.) and can be traded for local currencies on the Monetary Market — so real money can flow into the

wider economy.

42.2 How purchased Gold works

• 

Minted on purchase. The system creates Gold when bought; the operator is effectively the central bank of

Gold. Gold sinks (war costs, company creation/upgrades, fees, Pulse promotion) remove it from

circulation.

• 

Purchased Gold = earned Gold. Fully identical and fungible — no separate tracking of "paid" vs "free"

Gold.

• 

Pricing set by admin. The real-money price per Gold (and any tiered packages) is configured in the admin

panel and changeable at any time. The system supports tiered packages; actual prices are operator-set.

• 

Pay-to-win accepted. Buying Gold → converting to local currency → buying weapons/companies/power

is an intended, accepted tradeoff — the revenue model. (The anti-pay-to-win decisions elsewhere — no

Premium combat bonus, time-gated training/work — still stand; they limit certain advantages, but Gold

purchasing power is deliberately allowed.)

• 

No cash-out. Real money → Gold is one-way. Players can never withdraw real money from the system.

42.3 Payment system

The payment system (gateway integration) is deferred to implementation but is a required component of the

build, including regional payment methods for India / Brazil / Turkey / etc.

42.4 Admin Gold granting & oversight

• 

The Super Admin can grant Gold to any account directly from that player's profile (an "Add Gold" action)

— for refunds, compensation, promotions, manual purchase fulfillment, or events.

• 

Every Gold purchase and every admin Gold grant is logged. Server Admins can see the log showing that

a player purchased Gold (or was granted Gold), with amount, date, and source (real-money purchase vs

Super Admin grant). This ties into the admin audit system (§44) and the Gold-flow tracer (§43 AC-5) for

fraud detection.

43. Anti-Cheat

43.1 Philosophy

The anti-cheat system is flag-based: every detection adds to a moderation queue; no auto-punishments fire.

Systems are also designed so that a single undetected alt account cannot break balance (time-gated progression,

employee diminishing returns, salaries that cost real money, public transparency). Public logs let players police

each other (crowdsourced anti-cheat).

43.2 Economic funneling & real-money Gold (AC-1 to AC-5)

• 

AC-1 — Starter kit non-transferable for 7 days. The 5 Gold + 50 local currency + items a new account

receives cannot be donated, traded, or market-sold for the first 7 days. Kills "spawn many alts, harvest

starter kits."

• 

AC-2 — New-account donation lock. Donations from or to accounts younger than 7 days are blocked

entirely; new accounts cannot receive large donations until level 5. Stops fresh alts being used as Gold

mules.

• 

AC-3 — Market wash-trade detection. Flags when the same two accounts repeatedly trade (one always

buying the other's listings), or when items sell far above/below the market median. Goes to the mod queue.

• 

AC-4 — Same-payment-method linking flag. When multiple accounts buy real-money Gold from the

same payment method / card / wallet, they are flagged as linked (a strong alt signal).

• 

AC-5 — Gold-flow tracing. Every Gold transfer between accounts is traceable in an admin "Gold flow"

view, so moderators can follow the money (extends admin Gold-grant logging — §42).

43.3 Pulse manipulation (AC-6 to AC-9)

• 

AC-6 — Alt-cluster engagement excluded from Trending. Follows/likes from accounts in the same IP

cluster / payment cluster as the target do not count toward Trending — you can't boost your own fame with

your own alts.

• 

AC-7 — New/low account engagement carries zero ranking weight. Likes/follows from accounts

younger than ~3 days or below level 2 carry zero weight in Trending/For You ranking (they can still like

— the UI works — but it doesn't inflate rankings).

• 

AC-8 — Rapid-engagement burst detection. A statistically improbable burst of likes/follows from many

accounts in a short window is flagged for mod review rather than auto-trending.

• 

AC-9 — Payment-cluster coordinated-promotion flag. Flags when one payment-method cluster is

promoting coordinated political content (election-manipulation signal).

43.4 Political manipulation (AC-10 to AC-12)

• 

AC-10 — Voting requires recent activity. A voter must have logged in / acted at least 7 of the last 14

days to vote. Dormant alts farmed up once and abandoned cannot vote. (This is in addition to the level 7 +

30-day-citizenship gates.)

• 

AC-11 — Same-IP-cluster vote flag. If many votes in one election come from one IP cluster, the election

result is flagged for mod review (does not auto-void — mods investigate).

Feature Blueprint — Final

• 

AC-12 — Party-stacking detection. Flags when a party's membership surges with many new / low-

activity / same-cluster accounts right before the top-5 cutoff (artificial top-5 inflation).

43.5 Combat & economic farming (AC-13 to AC-15)

• 

AC-13 — Damage dominator flag. One account dealing >40% of a country's total damage in 7 days is

flagged.

• 

AC-14 — Coordinated-combat flag. Accounts in the same IP/payment cluster that repeatedly fight in the

same battles on the same side are flagged.

• 

AC-15 — Economy is alt-resistant by design. Time-gated work (1/day), employee diminishing returns

(§9.8), salaries that cost real money, and raw-material supply chains mean alt labor isn't free — the owner

pays salary + maintains raw materials. No additional rule needed; this is a deliberate design property.

43.6 Detection infrastructure & philosophy (AC-16 to AC-20)

• 

AC-16 — Suspicion scoring. Every flag (IP cluster, payment cluster, VPN, damage dominator, wash

trade, rapid engagement, etc.) adds to an account's suspicion score. High scores surface in the mod queue,

sorted by score. Mods investigate; nothing auto-bans.

• 

AC-17 — Admin investigation tools. An alt-cluster graph (accounts linked by IP, payment method,

device, behavior timing); the Gold-flow tracer; a per-account donation/trade history viewer; a shared-IP/

device viewer; and an action replay log (timeline of an account's actions).

• 

AC-18 — Behavioral / timing detection. Flags impossible timing (actions exactly every X seconds), 24/7

activity with no gaps, and perfectly synchronized multi-account actions (catches bots better than

CAPTCHAs).

• 

AC-19 — Public transparency as crowdsourced anti-cheat. Public country treasury logs, public

donations, public MU treasuries, the permanent public ban log, the public currency print log, and public

action logs let players police one another. This is a deliberate anti-cheat strategy.

• 

AC-20 — New-account friction. Email verification (required at registration), progressive feature

unlocking by level, and the level/citizenship gates on powerful systems all raise the cost of mass alt

creation.

The account is permanently deleted: account, companies, inventory, raw materials, treasury, newspaper, MU/party

membership, friendships, Pulse content — all deleted. Pending citizenship applications are cancelled. No

retention of statistical records.

50. Email Policy

50.1 Transactional emails (enabled)

Email verification at registration; password reset; account security alerts (new device login, password changed,

50.2 In-game notification emails (disabled)

50.3 Unsubscribe and preferences

All transactional emails except password reset and security alerts include unsubscribe links. Re-engagement

emails can be unsubscribed. Email preferences are manageable in account settings.

50.4 Click flows — managing email preferences

Email is transactional only — verification, password reset, security alerts, and a capped re-engagement email (no

login for 14 days, max 1 per 30 days). No in-game event ever emails you; those stay in the bell (§35 / §50.2).

Managing preferences. Top bar → settings cog → Email Preferences → toggle the optional transactional

categories and unsubscribe from re-engagement; password-reset and security-alert emails cannot be turned

off. Most transactional emails also carry an unsubscribe link in the footer that lands on this same preferences

page.
